Black Friday discount math in 60 seconds

Most stores pick a Black Friday discount by looking at competitors. The problem: a discount does not cut your profit by the same percentage. Your product cost, shipping and payment fees stay the same, so the whole discount comes out of the profit you keep on each order.

Take a $100 product that costs $50 to make, ship and process. At full price you keep $50. At 20% off you keep $30, so you need 1.67× the orders to make the same profit. At 30% off you keep $20 and need 2.5×. At 40% off you keep $10 and need 5×. A 40% sale has to quintuple your orders just to stand still.

Break-even ROAS goes up during a sale

If you run ads, the discount also raises the ROAS you need. The same $100 order breaks even at a ROAS of 2.0 at full price, 2.67 at 20% off and 3.5 at 30% off. An ad that is profitable at 2.5 today can lose money on Black Friday with no change to the creative, and that is the same week CPMs usually climb.

Offers that protect margin

Bundles, gift with purchase, free shipping thresholds and tiered offers ("spend $100, save $20") often beat a flat % off sitewide because they raise order value instead of only cutting price. Run each option through the calculator above and compare profit, not revenue.

How much more do I need to sell if I offer a discount?

Divide your profit per order at full price by your profit per order on sale. Example: a $100 product that costs $50 (product, shipping, fees) makes $50 profit. At 20% off it makes $30, so you need 50 ÷ 30 = 1.67× the orders. At 30% off you need 2.5×, at 40% off 5×.

What is break-even ROAS and how does a discount change it?

Break-even ROAS = order revenue ÷ profit per order before ad cost. A $100 order with $50 costs breaks even at a ROAS of 2.0. At 20% off ($80 revenue, $30 profit) it becomes 2.67; at 30% off ($70, $20) it becomes 3.5.

What is the maximum discount I can afford?

The discount where profit per order hits $0 after product cost, shipping, payment fees, ad cost and refunds. Past that point every extra sale loses money.

How the calculator works

Why does a 25% discount need way more than 25% more orders?

Your product, shipping and fees cost the same no matter the price. The discount comes straight out of the profit you keep on each order. Orders needed = profit per order at full price ÷ profit per order on sale.

What is the max safe discount?

The discount where each order exactly covers product cost, shipping, payment fees, ad cost and refunds. Go deeper and every sale loses money, no matter how many you make.

What is break-even ROAS?

The return on ad spend where ads cost exactly what the order earns before ads: price ÷ profit per order before ad cost. A discount shrinks that profit, so your break-even ROAS goes up during the sale.

How are returns handled?

The return rate removes that share of revenue while product, shipping and fees are still paid. That's a conservative assumption (some returned items can be resold).
